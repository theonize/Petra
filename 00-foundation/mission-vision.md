# Mission & Vision

**Status:** draft  
**Owner:** Worship Lead / Pastoral oversight  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-13  
**Review cadence:** annual

## Mission

Help the church **sing** — encounter God through music, word, and well-run technical craft — so the room can respond without distraction.

## Primary intent (non-negotiable)

**The point of musical worship leadership is to encourage the congregation to sing.**

Everything else is downstream of that:

| If this is true… | Then we… |
|---|---|
| The room is singing | Keys, volume, arrangement, and ego all serve the people |
| The room is watching a show | We have drifted — simplify, turn down, make space, re-center the lyric |
| Only the platform sounds full | We failed the assignment, even if the band felt great |

Talent, production, and creativity are gifts **for** congregational voice — not replacements for it.

## Vision

A team that is:

- **Spiritually grounded** — servants first, musicians second
- **Musically prepared** — charts learned, parts clear, dynamics intentional
- **Technically invisible** — sound, lights, and media serve the moment
- **Artistically free** — mundane decisions already settled, so presence and craft can breathe
- **Relationally safe** — people can grow, fail forward, and stay

## What success looks like (signals, not scoreboard)

- The **congregation sings** (primary signal)
- Transitions feel calm
- Teammates know expectations before Sunday
- New volunteers can be onboarded without tribal knowledge
- Artists are not burning cycles on avoidable logistics
- Gear and people are cared for after the last song

## Non-goals

- Platform culture or celebrity musicianship
- Complexity that only the same three people can run
- Policies that exist to control rather than free the team
- A “tight show” that quiets the room

See [scope-and-authority.md](scope-and-authority.md): the runbook holds the **mundane framework** (call times, patches, swaps, checklists) so artists and tech can show up free to lead worship and help the church sing.

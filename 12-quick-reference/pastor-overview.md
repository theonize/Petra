# Petra for Pastors — Framework Highlights

**Status:** draft  
**Owner:** Worship Lead + Pastoral oversight  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-20  
**Review cadence:** annual

Two pages for pastors and elders: what Petra is, what it decides, where you are in the loop, and where it defers to you. Links point into the full runbook.

## What Petra is

Petra is the worship team's runbook — the **mundane framework** (schedules, rehearsal rules, charts, patches, slides, gear care) settled once so musicians and tech are free to lead worship. Named after the rock-cut city: durable structure carved so the team can worship freely inside it. "Petra is not a box, it is a skeleton" ([scope-and-authority](../00-foundation/scope-and-authority.md)).

Everything in it serves one sentence:

> **Encourage the congregation to sing.** ([mission-vision](../00-foundation/mission-vision.md))

The service gives people an hour to attend to Someone besides themselves. Musical worship may be a distraction **from the world**; it must never be a distraction **from God**, and it is not about the people on the platform. If the room is watching a show, the team's instruction is: simplify, turn down, step back, re-center the lyric ([values](../00-foundation/values.md)).

## What it governs — and what it does not

| Petra governs | Petra does **not** govern |
|---|---|
| Team culture and expectations | Church employee handbook / HR |
| Musician, vocalist, tech procedures | Facility master calendar |
| Scheduling norms and swap rules | **Doctrinal statements** — owned by elders/pastors |
| Rehearsal and service run-of-show standards | **Child protection master text** — Petra complies and points to it |
| Music library, song selection, licensing awareness, chart conventions | Budget approval authority — follows church finance process |
| Equipment care owned by the worship/tech ministry | **Artistic calling in the moment** — dynamics, prayer, spontaneous response within the plan and pastoral covering |
| Onboarding paths; shared language | |

Design rule for every page: "must" only for safety, care, or congregational clarity; checklists for repeatable risk, not scripts that kill presence; every exception has a named decider.

## Five habits that make it work

1. **Sunday starts midweek** — personal practice before rehearsal; group rehearsal before the service
2. **No prior rehearsal → no serve** (default) — band, vocals, **and** tech; accepting a slot includes the rehearsal tied to it
3. **Rehearse the way we serve** — house lyrics and confidence monitors run at midweek rehearsal, not only Sunday
4. **One plan, published early** — the Song Selector fixes the set by a weekly deadline; everyone prepares from it
5. **Eyes up** — confidence monitors are the default, music stands the exception

## How songs are chosen

The [song-selection-policy](../06-music-library/song-selection-policy.md) is the part of Petra most directly under pastoral interest.

- **Six core criteria — every song passes all six:** biblically defensible theology · corporate-worship intent · congregational ownership · congregational singability · clear Christian confession · pastoral usefulness
- **Hard boundaries:** no secular or generic love songs in the worship set; the congregation never sings in God's first-person voice; a **50-title rolling 12-month ceiling** (a maximum, not a target — a smaller repertoire known deeply is preferred)
- **Continuity:** at least one song carries over from the preceding week; Worship Lead may waive with a recorded reason
- **Teaching:** one unfamiliar song per service; ~3 outings in 6–8 weeks; known songs carry new ones; across the year the diet covers the whole Christian vocabulary — adoration, creation, incarnation, cross, resurrection and reign, Spirit, confession, assurance, thanksgiving, lament, petition, mission, communion, baptism, hope
- **Pastoral touchpoints:** theology-sensitive or disputed lyrics go to pastoral oversight before use; the new-song gate requires a line-by-line lyric review; the **annual repertoire review is done with pastoral oversight** to confirm doctrinal alignment and find gaps in the church's sung theology
- **Lifecycle:** Scout → Trial → Core → Rest → Archive ([song-lifecycle](../06-music-library/song-lifecycle.md)); register kept in the [annual-song-repertoire](../10-templates/annual-song-repertoire.md)

## Who owns what

| Role | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Worship Lead** | Spiritual and operational owner; approves policy; final accountability for Sunday readiness and song diet |
| **Song Selector** | Chooses **what** we sing and why; publishes the fixed set; hands off to Director |
| **Director** | Owns **how** it is played — keys, forms, charts, rehearsal |
| **Tech Lead** | Owns production standards and tech roster health |
| Band, vocals, FOH, monitors, media, lights, stream, stage | Each has a one-page charter in [01-roles/](../01-roles/README.md) |

One person may wear several hats; each hat's must-dos still apply.

## Where pastors are in the loop

| Decision | Propose | Decide |
|---|---|---|
| Songs / setlist titles for a service | Song Selector | Worship Lead |
| Keys, tempos, forms, arrangements | Director | Director (Worship Lead if sensitive) |
| **Appoint Director, Song Selector, or Tech Lead** | Worship Lead | **Pastoral oversight** |
| **Change call times permanently** | Worship Lead | Worship Lead + **pastor liaison** |
| Major gear purchase | Tech Lead | **Church finance / pastor process** |
| One-week exception to an active policy | Role owner | Worship Lead |
| Edit an active policy | Anyone | Worship Lead (or designee) — announced via changelog |

**Escalation:** peer → Director / Tech Lead → Worship Lead → **pastoral oversight**. Pastoral-care and safety issues skip the chain. Mission, vision, and scope pages name pastoral oversight as co-owner ([scope-and-authority](../00-foundation/scope-and-authority.md)).

**On a Sunday:** the platform lead listens to the pastor for deviations; if the sermon or prayer extends, the band watches the leader rather than inventing a concert. Incidents: people safety first, preserve the moment second, communicate once on a known channel ([service-run-of-show](../04-service/service-run-of-show.md) · [incidents](../04-service/incidents-and-contingencies.md)). Series notes and pastoral themes are the first input to each week's set.

## The biblical baseline

[freedom-in-form-study](../00-foundation/freedom-in-form-study.md) (Jacob, the Levites, Ephesians 5:15–21) supplies the conviction behind the framework — a teaching draft, not doctrine; pastoral leadership owns final doctrinal language.

> Order does not manufacture spiritual freedom. Christ gives freedom, and the Spirit gives life. Faithful order can protect that freedom by removing avoidable confusion, distributing service, guarding the church's shared confession, and making room for the congregation to sing.

Safeguards the study places on Petra itself: Christ, not competence, gives access to God · Scripture judges the system (Mark 7:6–13) · love governs freedom · order serves edification (1 Cor 14) · **pastoral authority and operational authority are distinct** · exceptions must be possible and owned · the congregation's voice is evidence, not an idol — silence, lament, prayer, and sacrament are also faithful responses.

## Signals of health vs drift

| Healthy | Drifting |
|---|---|
| The congregation sings (primary signal) | The room is watching a show |
| Transitions feel calm; Sunday feels shepherded | Only the platform sounds full |
| Teammates know the plan before Sunday | Complexity only three people can run |
| New volunteers onboard without tribal knowledge | Policies that control rather than free |
| Gear and people are cared for after the last song | A "tight show" that quiets the room |

## How to ask for a change

Anyone may propose; the Worship Lead decides `active` policy; material changes are discussed before merge and logged in the [CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md).

## What Petra is

Petra is the worship team’s runbook — the **mundane framework** (schedules, rehearsal rules, charts, patches, slides, gear care) settled once so musicians and tech are free to lead worship. “Petra is not a box, it is a skeleton” ([Scope & Authority](../00-foundation/scope-and-authority.md)). It is generic: every church-specific value (names, clocks, tools, safeguarding links) lives in one fill-in page, [Our Congregation](../10-templates/our-congregation.md).

Everything in it serves one sentence:

> **Encourage the congregation to sing.** ([Mission & Vision](../00-foundation/mission-vision.md))

The service gives people an hour to attend to Someone besides themselves. Musical worship may be a distraction **from the world**; it must never be a distraction **from God**, and it is not about the people on the platform. If the room is watching a show, the team’s instruction is: simplify, turn down, step back, re-center the lyric ([Values](../00-foundation/values.md)). Craft removes avoidable obstacles; it does not manufacture God’s presence.

## What it governs — and what it does not

| Petra governs | Petra does **not** govern |
|---|---|
| Team culture and expectations | Church employee handbook / HR |
| Musician, vocalist, tech procedures | Facility master calendar |
| Scheduling norms and swap rules | **Doctrine** — owned by pastors/elders |
| Rehearsal and service run-of-show standards | **Protection / safeguarding policy** — Petra adheres to yours and states a worship-ministry baseline |
| Music library, song selection, licensing awareness, chart conventions | Budget approval authority — follows church finance process |
| Equipment care owned by the worship/tech ministry | **Artistic calling in the moment** — dynamics, prayer, spontaneous response within the plan and pastoral covering |
| Onboarding paths; shared language | |

## Five habits that make it work

1. **Sunday starts midweek** — personal practice before rehearsal; a 90-minute group rehearsal that works every song and transition and ends with a full run
2. **No prior rehearsal → no serve** (default) — band, vocals, **and** tech; exceptions are granted in advance, not assumed
3. **Rehearse the way we serve** — house lyrics and confidence monitors run at midweek rehearsal, not only Sunday
4. **One plan, published early** — the Song Selector proposes the set by a weekly deadline; the Director confirms (silence counts); the Worship Lead publishes keys and charts by a second deadline
5. **Eyes up** — confidence monitors are the default, music stands the exception

## How songs are chosen

The [Song Selection Policy](../06-music-library/song-selection-policy.md) is the part of Petra most directly under pastoral interest.

- **Six core criteria — every song passes all six:** biblically defensible theology · corporate-worship intent · congregational ownership · congregational singability · clear Christian confession · pastoral usefulness
- **We avoid** secular or generic love songs in the worship set, and the congregation singing in God’s first-person voice. These are avoidances, not bans: **leadership — Director with pastoral oversight — may make a documented exception** (e.g. a Scripture chorus with a speaker cue). A **50-title rolling 12-month ceiling** keeps the repertoire deep rather than wide
- **Continuity and teaching:** at least one song carries over each week; one unfamiliar song per service, ~3 outings in 6–8 weeks; across the year the diet covers the whole Christian vocabulary (adoration to lament to hope)
- **Pastoral touchpoints:** theology-sensitive or disputed lyrics go to pastoral oversight before use; the new-song gate requires a line-by-line lyric review; the **annual repertoire review is done with pastoral oversight** to confirm doctrinal alignment and find gaps in the church’s sung theology
- **Lifecycle:** Scout → Trial → Core → Rest → Archive ([Song Lifecycle](../06-music-library/song-lifecycle.md)); register kept in the [Annual Repertoire Register](../10-templates/annual-song-repertoire.md)

## Who owns what

| Role | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Director** | Owns the ministry under the pastor; appoints the team leads; approves policy; **accountable for the congregation’s worship and the team’s health** (≈ worship pastor) |
| **Worship Lead** | Owns **how** it is played — keys, forms, charts, rehearsal, musical calls on Sunday (≈ music director); usually the **Platform lead** the band watches |
| **Song Selector** | Chooses **what** we sing and why; proposes the set; hands off to the Worship Lead |
| **Tech Lead** | Owns production standards and tech roster health |
| Band, vocals, FOH, monitors, media, lights, stream, stage | Each has a one-page charter in [Roles](../01-roles/README.md) |

One person may wear several hats; each hat’s must-dos still apply.

## Where pastors are in the loop

| Decision | Propose | Decide |
|---|---|---|
| Songs / setlist titles for a service | Song Selector (proposed set by deadline) | Director confirms; silence by the confirm-by time = confirmed |
| Keys, tempos, forms, arrangements | Worship Lead | Worship Lead (Director if sensitive) |
| **Appoint the Director** | Pastor | **Pastor** |
| Appoint Worship Lead, Song Selector, or Tech Lead | Director | Director (**pastor consulted**) |
| **Change call times permanently** | Director | Director + **pastor (or delegate)** |
| Exception to a song-selection avoidance | Song Selector / Director | **Director with pastoral oversight**, recorded |
| Major gear purchase | Tech Lead | **Church finance / pastor process** |
| One-week exception to a policy · edit a policy | Role owner · anyone | Director (or delegate) — logged in the changelog |

**Line of authority:** Pastor → Director → team leads. The pastor has final say; responsibility and accountability for the congregation’s worship rest with the Director. **Escalation:** section lead → Worship Lead / Tech Lead → Director → **pastor**; care and safety issues skip the chain ([Scope & Authority](../00-foundation/scope-and-authority.md)).

**On a Sunday:** the Platform lead listens to the pastor for deviations; if the sermon or prayer extends, the band watches the Platform lead rather than inventing a concert. **Safety:** people first — any lead may stop the music for a person; the team adheres to your protection policy and has the designated reporter’s name before serving ([Safety & Protection](../08-culture-care/safety-and-protection.md) · [Incidents & Contingencies](../04-service/incidents-and-contingencies.md)). Series notes and pastoral themes are the first input to each week’s set.

## The biblical baseline

[Freedom in Form study](../00-foundation/freedom-in-form-study.md) (Jacob, the Levites, Ephesians 5 / Colossians 3) supplies the conviction behind the framework — a teaching draft, not doctrine; pastoral leadership owns final doctrinal language and is asked to review its citations.

> Order does not manufacture spiritual freedom. Christ gives freedom, and the Spirit gives life. Faithful order can protect that freedom by removing avoidable confusion, distributing service, guarding the church’s shared confession, and making room for the congregation to sing.

Safeguards the study places on Petra itself: Christ, not competence, gives access to God · Scripture judges the system · love governs freedom · order serves edification · **pastoral authority and operational authority are distinct** · exceptions must be possible and owned · the congregation’s voice is evidence, not an idol.

## Signals of health vs drift

**Healthy:** the congregation sings (primary signal) · transitions feel calm, Sunday feels shepherded · teammates know the plan before Sunday · new volunteers onboard without tribal knowledge. **Drifting:** the room is watching a show · only the platform sounds full · complexity only three people can run · policies that control rather than free.
